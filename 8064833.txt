Project Topic
Design and simulate a mobile robot that patrols university corridors, approaches noticeboards, captures high-resolution poster images, and performs information extraction
and analysis to detect expired or outdated announcements and generate structured reports.

Problem Definition & Motivation
Highly used buildings (e.g., Architecture, EEF) contain many noticeboards whose content changes
frequently. Manually checking for expired or duplicate announcements is tedious. We propose an
autonomous system that safely navigates crowded corridors, aligns to boards, reads posters, flags
anomalies (e.g., past-due dates), and logs results for facility staff.

Environment & Platform
• Simulation: Gazebo Harmonic indoor corridor with multiple noticeboards, benches, moving
pedestrians.
• Robot platform: TurtleBot-class differential drive robot equipped with a 2D LiDAR for
mapping and obstacle avoidance, and an RGB or RGB-D camera for board detection and
image capture. ROS2 Humble will be used.

High-Level Requirements
1. Safe Navigation & Approach: Build or obtain a 2D map, localize, avoid pedestrians, and
dock to a board viewing pose.
2. Perception & Information Processing: Identify and localize noticeboards, capture images,
and process visual data to extract relevant content such as titles, dates, and general summaries.
Detect potential anomalies including outdated or duplicated announcements. Methods may
involve image-based detection, fiducial markers or external services.
3. Data Logging: Record board IDs, timestamps, captured images, extracted fields, and anomaly
flags for reporting and traceability.
4. Testing & Metrics: Evaluate success rate, navigation safety, approach accuracy, perception robustness, and information analysis precision/recall, including demonstration through
recorded video presentations.

Method & Approach
Perception (Boards and Posters)
• Board and poster observation: The robot identifies noticeboards, aligns its view, and
captures high-quality images ensuring sufficient framing, resolution, and lighting.
• Information extraction and analysis: Captured visual data is processed or transmitted to
obtain relevant content such as titles, dates, or summaries. Potentially outdated or duplicated
announcements are flagged. The method may involve onboard processing or external services.
• Optional refinement: When confidence in extracted information is low, secondary reasoning
or external interpretation modules may be applied to improve accuracy.

Localization and Mapping
• The robot constructs an internal map of the environment, identifying corridors and board
positions.
• During operation, it continuously updates its own position on this map, allowing it to navigate
accurately and approach each board safely.

Planning and Control
• The robot plans efficient and safe routes between all target boards, considering environmental
constraints such as corridor width and pedestrian movement.
• Motion control adjusts velocity and direction dynamically to avoid collisions and maintain
smooth navigation.
• Upon reaching a board, the robot stops at a suitable viewing distance and adjusts its orientation to face the board frontally.
• The full behavior sequence follows a structured routine: navigate to board → align view →
capture image → extract and analyze text → log findings → proceed to next board.
